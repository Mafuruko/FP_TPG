# Game design

## Confirmed direction

Island Colony is a peaceful, small-scale colony simulator on a mysterious tropical island. The player starts with about five villagers and gradually builds a home for up to about forty. Individual villagers should feel recognizable through their appearance, work, families and life stages.

The intended loop is to gather resources, build facilities, grow the village, develop skills, unlock abilities, and explore the island. Player-directed work uses individual villagers and environmental targets. Villager skill belongs to each person. Ability unlocks apply to the whole village. Appropriate tools appear through the existing activity animations.

The economy tracks Food, Wood, Stone and Knowledge. Berries, crops and fish feed the shared Food counter. Existing object, item and water-body relationships determine what the player sees when a villager gathers something.

The Collection Book contains ten seashells, five minerals, five ancient artifacts and five butterflies. Ordinary collectibles are independent of the monument. Wildlife primarily gives the island movement and variety. Fishing spots remain independent of visible fish. The clock cycles through day and night, and weather alternates between clear conditions and rain.

## Playable progression

Houses make room for more villagers. A Family House makes it possible for eligible adults to start a family. Gathering, fishing, farming, healing, construction and research give villagers useful work. Villagers age, and their hunger, energy and health affect their routines. A newborn temporarily takes one adult away from work.

The campfire, spring, garden, bridge, cave, waterfall and monument each have a playable project. The journal explains the requirements. Restore the monument to earn the island restoration achievement and continue tending the village. Yields, costs, and simulation timings are an initial balance pass.

## Future design

Achievements and proficiency support players who enjoy goals. Hidden places and species reward exploration. Families and individual identities support attachment to villagers. Solo challenge goals can reward competitive play without requiring multiplayer or combat. These are single-player adaptations of Bartle's player motivations.

Optional hunting and minor wildlife hazards remain possible expansions. The current landmark chain can be extended with additional environmental clues and exploration rewards.
